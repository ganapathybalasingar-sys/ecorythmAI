 Ecorythm AI — Technical Research

Researching intelligent, resource-aware environmental decision-making.

About

Ecorythm AI is a proposed AI-powered sustainability platform designed to help users evaluate environmental interventions under financial and resource constraints.

This repository documents the research, technical references, algorithms, datasets, and design decisions that inform its EcoSim Engine.

Research Objectives

Study environmental simulation and scenario modelling.

Explore budget-constrained optimisation algorithms.

Research resource allocation and feasibility checking.

Investigate greenhouse gas emissions, water conservation, and waste reduction calculations.

Design transparent and reproducible sustainability simulations.

Explore how generative AI can explain simulation results.

Core Research Areas

1. Environmental Simulation

Study how environmental baselines, intervention scenarios, and projected outcomes are modelled.

2. Budget Optimisation

Explore methods for selecting interventions within a fixed financial budget.

3. Resource Allocation

Investigate constraints involving land, labour, water, equipment, and implementation capacity.

4. Environmental Impact Assessment

Research emissions factors, water-saving estimates, waste diversion, and uncertainty.

5. AI-Assisted Decision Support

Explore how large language models can explain model outputs without replacing scientific calculations.

Proposed Technology Stack

Frontend: React

Backend: Python and FastAPI

Database: SQLite

AI assistance: Gemini API

Simulation: Python-based calculation engine

Optimisation: Mathematical optimisation methods, selected according to the problem


Research Principles

Distinguish measured data from assumptions and estimates.

Record the source and units of environmental factors.

Validate budget and resource constraints.

Document uncertainty and limitations.

Respect the licences of external code and datasets.

Project Status

Research and design documentation in progress. Simulation capabilities should be considered planned until implemented and tested.

Contribution

Contributions may include research summaries, verified references, reproducible experiments, and technical design improvements.

Disclaimer

Environmental outputs are estimates dependent on the quality of input data, assumptions, and model limitations. They should not be interpreted as independently verified environmental results.
